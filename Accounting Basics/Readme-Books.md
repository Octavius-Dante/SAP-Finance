In traditional accounting, accounts are classified into three main types—Real, Personal, and Nominal Accounts, each following a specific "Golden Rule" for debit and credit.


1. Real Account

Real accounts relate to the assets and properties owned by a business. They do not close at the end of the year and carry their balances forward to the next.

Types: Tangible (can be touched, like cash, land, buildings, and machinery) and Intangible (cannot be touched, like goodwill and patents).

Golden Rule: Debit what comes in, credit what goes out.

Example: Buying machinery for cash means you debit the Machinery Account (comes in) and credit the Cash Account (goes out).


2. Personal Account

Personal accounts relate to individuals, firms, companies, or institutions with whom the business interacts. This includes customers, vendors, capital accounts, and bank accounts.

Types: Natural persons (real humans), artificial persons (companies, banks), and representative persons (outstanding expenses, prepaid income).

Golden Rule: Debit the receiver, credit the giver.

Example: Paying cash to a supplier named John means you debit John's Account (the receiver) and credit Cash.


3. Nominal Account

Nominal accounts are temporary accounts used to track expenses, losses, incomes, and gains. Their balances are closed at the end of the fiscal year and transferred to the profit and loss statement.

Types: Revenue/income accounts and expense/loss accounts.

Golden Rule: Debit all expenses and losses, credit all incomes and gains.

Example: Paying office rent means you debit the Rent Expense Account (expense) and credit Cash.
